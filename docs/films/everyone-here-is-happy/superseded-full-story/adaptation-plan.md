# Everyone Here Is Happy five minute adaptation

Superseded whole-story proposal, October 16, 2026. Based on Josh's [original story map](../original-story-map.md), preserved unchanged. The current short covers only the opening; see [the opening adaptation](../opening-short/adaptation-plan.md).

The short follows Ethan through a seemingly winnable rescue, then makes him watch the life Sarah lived while he was searching. Its emotional center is the interval between his relief at seeing her rescued and his recognition that he missed everything afterward.

The draft keeps the kind Archives, the faceless Playways, and the unresolved metropolitan ending. It gives approximately seventy seconds to the interview and playback. The search occupies enough time to establish Ethan's determination, but its individual adventures are compressed.

## Adaptation choices

| Element | Treatment in this draft |
|---|---|
| Hope forward and horror in retrospect | Warm, inviting Playways photography; forward-moving adventure music; no aging or calendar clues during the search. |
| Ethan is almost present | Coffee, light sensitivity, a phone interruption, and a missed incident at the ball pit. He actively puts the phone away when he notices the problem. |
| Fear of replacement | One phone message names Mark as an available alternative. Ethan's response is to look up and try to participate. No expository account of the marriage. |
| Sarah's specificity | A small habit: two taps before she commits to an action. A yellow star-shaped wrist token becomes Ethan's tangible connection to her. Both are new adaptation details. |
| Bully and disappearance | One brief, readable act of meanness, followed by crying and disappearance. No implication that Sarah is killed or mauled. |
| Ethan's voice | No voiceover. A defended man is understood through behavior and a few direct lines. This sidesteps the novel's narration fork without resolving it for the book. |
| Search as tracking | A discarded wrist token, a genuine recorded laugh, a child-height mark, and a departure record guide him. No puzzle-solving montage. |
| Pincer | When he stops, a zone closes around him; motion restores light and a route. The second guest and full predator demonstration are omitted for time. |
| False midpoint | An empty departure bay and Sarah's genuine participation record become evidence that he is close. The system promises no reunion. |
| Archives | A person can offer a cup and speak offscreen. This is routine care, not an antagonist confrontation. |
| Sarah's happy life | Separate candid scenes at several ages. She participates in her own life; none are messages to Ethan. |
| Ending | A public city that functions without attending to him. He sees possible routes, but we cut before he chooses. |

The token is found once, not replenished. Sarah wears it in her earlier participation picture but has a bare wrist in the rescue footage; Ethan keeps the actual object. The recorded laugh is an actual trace of Sarah's visit, replayed by the venue. The mark and record are also historical. Every statement by the Playways must survive a literal truth check. Avoid present-tense claims such as “she is waiting in the next room.”

## Confirmed timing direction

Josh selected the version in which Sarah has been seen recently and her later travels also alter her aging timeline. Keep the 120 outside years for Ethan. Sarah leaves early, grows up, then travels through routes with different elapsed-time relationships. She therefore remains an adult at her most recent recorded crossing, proposed here as three outside years before the interview. That sighting establishes a possible person to search for; it does not establish her current location or guarantee that she is still alive.

Sarah's approximately one subjective year inside remains compatible with the fifteen outside minutes at extraction. The short does not explain that local duration or the physics. The film needs the audience to understand that she escaped early and that Ethan lost a lifetime; it does not need them to calculate an exchange rate.

The playback shows childhood, young adulthood, a shared home, and later travel. It does not age her into a completed lifespan or imply a death. The line “She kept traveling. Her last crossing was three years ago” connects the recent record to her movement between worlds. Repeated gateway settings support that connection visually; a timed animatic should check that viewers understand it without a physics lecture.

## Protecting the reveal

Before the Archives, the audience may fear that the place is dangerous. They should still believe that finding Sarah is both necessary and possible. No aged toys, old dates, external clocks, premature electronic glitches, mocking voices, or ominous shots of a smiling face.

The search should contain tangible accomplishments: Ethan finds a real object, correctly follows a trace, crosses a difficult space, and reaches the place Sarah departed. Each success moves him forward. His competence earns the audience's support.

At the Archives, first show Sarah being helped. Hold Ethan's relief long enough for it to register. Only then show her older. Do not put a date or the 120-year line ahead of that visual transition. The viewer discovers the missing life through recognizable changes in Sarah, with the number arriving afterward.

Do not depict Sarah looking into the recording device and addressing her father. Keep her attention on the people beside her. Do not add a final accusation, apology, or message explaining her feelings about him. A happy life does not prove that she never remembered him, and the film need not claim access to that interior certainty. Ethan sees a life that did not depend on his return.

The Archives' final line, “This happens a few times per cycle,” should be delivered with ordinary care. An evil inflection would change the institution and weaken the ending. The cup, chair, tissue, and open door are sufficient evidence of practiced assistance.

## Visual and sound bible for the first pass

**Ethan:** one adult identity throughout. A simple charcoal overshirt, neutral T-shirt, dark trousers, and worn trainers provide trackable costume shapes. Dirt and a small sleeve tear accumulate; his face does not age during the search. Exhaustion grows through posture and breathing. These are proposed visual choices.

**Sarah:** establish the child before producing older versions. Preserve a small set of visible identity traits across separately approved age references: eye spacing, nose shape, smile asymmetry, and hair texture. Wardrobe changes with her life. The latest proposed appearance is a mature adult, around forty, rather than elderly. These apparent ages are production reference targets, not a fixed conversion between subjective and outside time. The wrist token belongs to the child visit; it must not become a magical object worn throughout her life.

**The Playways:** saturated but photographed as a usable place. Soft structural forms, diffuse practical light, primary-color routes, and impossible scale. Three reusable environments are enough: a ball basin, a tube/bridge concourse, and a party/departure bay. Avoid filling every frame with unrelated surreal objects.

**The Archives:** clear, comfortable light; furniture sized for a person; no interrogation spotlight, cages, or sinister uniforms. Use one seat, one display plane, and one offscreen attendant. The city beyond should feel inhabited and indifferent, not an apocalyptic ruin.

**Sound:** real party noise opens the film. A welcoming venue sound carries across the threshold. A modest adventure motif supports Ethan's progress. When Sarah is rescued on the screen, preserve relief before thinning the score. Her later records contain ordinary laughter, company, and place sound. Under Ethan's final reaction, retain the room's ventilation and the small sound of a cup being placed down. Do not use a villain sting.

Keep two tap sounds as one reusable audio motif. They should first be Sarah's personal rhythm, then a recognizable behavior in the life records. Reusing an identifiable gesture is more reliable than relying entirely on generated facial aging to tell the audience whom they are seeing.

## What this story changes about the workflow plan

This story has two principal characters, but it exceeds the earlier generic two-character production assumption. Sarah needs several age references. The records also need a small number of companions, and the film needs a party, several Playways spaces, an archive room, and a city. Keep background faces incidental and reserve identity work for Ethan and Sarah.

| Workflow role | Story-specific job | First validation |
|---|---|---|
| Character reference generation | Ethan plus child, adolescent, young-adult, and mature-adult Sarah references | Can viewers recognize Sarah across three ages without captions? |
| Environment references | Reusable party, three Playways areas, Archives, and city compositions | Geography and light remain stable in two angles of one environment |
| Reference-based keyframes | Place Ethan consistently in the route; establish Sarah's life records | Face, costume, hands, and composition are acceptable before animation |
| H3 I2V or R2V shots | Ethan's search, short actions, and reactions | One action reads clearly with little unintended camera movement |
| Controlled endpoint shots | A bounded threshold crossing where both ends are known | No morphing of Ethan or the structure |
| Age progression stills | Distinct Sarah references for records | Each age is approved as a person, not an in-shot aging effect |
| Archive footage | Short candid moments at separately approved ages | Social interaction reads as ordinary and happy |
| Screen compositing | Put approved footage onto the archive display | Perspective, exposure, and timing match the reaction plate |
| Dialogue and performance | Few Ethan lines, a gentle venue voice, the attendant | Exact words and believable timing; offscreen lines can use clean audio |
| Editorial and sound | Reveal timing, match cuts, continuous ambience, final title | Meaning is clear at animatic resolution before expensive finishing |

Do not ask one generated clip to age Sarah through a lifetime. Generate discrete records and cut between them. Do not ask a video model to reproduce a moving film accurately inside the archive screen. Generate the screen content and the room plate separately, then composite. For the first pass, direct cuts between full-frame records and Ethan's reaction can carry most of the sequence with only one screen-in-room establishing composite.

Likewise, add text in post: the phone message, Sarah's departure label, the 120-year record if needed, and the title. Keep all pre-reveal records free of visible dates. Node or model choices should not introduce information the screenplay has deliberately withheld.

The existing three-clip continuation workflow is useful for an individual uninterrupted passage. It should not connect the entire search. Hard cuts between distinct locations make space for performance, preserve independent retakes, and let the edit imply a longer journey.

## The most useful first tests

The earlier research proposed a general 30-second pilot. This story benefits more from testing its particular failure points before building every workflow.

1. **A silent identity contact sheet.** Approve Ethan and Sarah at three ages. Ask a viewer unfamiliar with the plan whether the Sarah images read as one person at different ages. Refine before animation.
2. **A 20–30 second reveal animatic.** Child rescued, Ethan relieved, older Sarah laughing, Ethan unable to speak. Temporary stills and recorded scratch sound are enough to judge whether the transition carries the loss. This is the emotional feasibility test.
3. **A short search sequence.** Three shots: a trace found, a threshold crossed, a determined reaction. Test consistent Ethan, wardrobe, and screen direction. This is the generation and continuity test.
4. **One ten-second reaction.** Ethan watches, tries to smile, and fails to finish an inhale. If the model overacts or changes his face, simplify performance and framing before making a film dependent on long close-ups.

A technically smooth search cannot rescue an unreadable final playback. Spend the earliest review effort on identity and the reveal.

## Production boundaries

The script and manifest are drafts. They contain proposed imagery and timing; they do not contain approved generation assets or executable ComfyUI graphs. No generation budget has been set and no Cloud jobs have been submitted for this story.

The next writing pass should refine Ethan and Sarah's specificity in the opening and check that the recent-sighting line leaves room for the loss to land. The ending now asks whether Ethan can seek the adult Sarah without simply restarting his pursuit of the four-year-old. After a timed read and an animatic, convert the selected shot descriptions into model-specific prompts. Preserve the dramatic text separately so changes made to improve model behavior cannot quietly rewrite the story.
