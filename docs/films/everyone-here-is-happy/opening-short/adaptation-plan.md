# Everyone Here Is Happy opening short

Draft scope confirmed October 16, 2026: birthday party, Sarah disappearing into the ball pit, Ethan going after her, and his arrival in the Playways. Target length: five minutes including a ten-second end-title allocation. Based on Josh's [story map](../original-story-map.md).

The film's turning point is the moment Ethan discovers that the ball pit no longer belongs to the room above it. Its final image begins the rescue journey. The audience leaves wanting him to find Sarah, with no explanation of what the Playways will eventually cost him.

## Dramatic shape

Give the ordinary world enough time to matter. Sarah is a particular child with a relationship to Ethan before she becomes the person he is looking for. Ethan is distracted and uncomfortable, but he can make her laugh and knows her habits. When he realizes something is wrong, he acts immediately.

The disappearance should initially look like a problem that adults can solve. Staff respond, children are moved out, and Ethan searches where he last saw her. The impossible space is discovered through that sensible search. This progression lets the audience enter the Playways with him.

| Time | Sequence | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| 00:00–01:15 | Father and daughter at the birthday party | Establish affection, Sarah's specificity, Ethan's distraction, and a hint of his fear of replacement |
| 01:15–02:05 | Ball-pit incident and disappearance | A small harm becomes a problem he must fix |
| 02:05–03:10 | Adults search the pit | Escalate from ordinary worry to the discovery of an impossible gap |
| 03:10–03:55 | Ethan follows | A deliberate protective choice becomes the threshold crossing |
| 03:55–04:50 | Arrival in the Playways | Reveal impossible scale, offer a route, and begin the quest |
| 04:50–05:00 | End title | Let the arrival settle |

These are animatic targets. If the party or search plays slowly, tighten it before rendering rather than padding a performance to meet a number.

## The first draft

The [screenplay](screenplay-v01.fountain) gives Sarah a small starting ritual: two taps and a request for Ethan to say “ready.” She also puts a crooked paper crown on his head. He briefly joins her play, then his phone takes him away. These are proposed specifics for this adaptation, not details taken from the original bible.

A single message from Lena names Mark as an alternative caregiver. It supplies the splinter of replaceability without explaining the separation. Ethan does not deliver a speech about being a bad father. His choice to put the phone away, a beat too late, is enough to begin the wound.

The mean child takes a foam step from Sarah. She cries, goes below the balls, briefly reaches up, and is gone. Nothing visibly attacks her. Staff help. The scene does not depend on every adult except Ethan behaving implausibly.

Ethan finds Sarah's yellow wrist star while searching. He pockets it before crossing and keeps it on arrival. It is one physical object with a continuous path, not a clue that the system duplicates. The star is another proposed production detail and can be replaced with a more personal object later.

The threshold is a seam in the padded back wall that recedes farther than it should. Ethan follows it because it is the only route he has found. We do not show where Sarah went or give the audience a separate view of her situation.

The Playways reveal is bright, immense, and navigable. Ethan calls her name. A route lights. A pleasant welcome is his only response. He takes a first step toward it, and the film ends.

## What the opening must preserve

The viewer stays with Ethan's knowledge. No cutaways to Sarah being rescued, no time indicators, no older Sarah, no Archives, and no glimpse of an operator controlling the place. The later nonlinear-time decision remains in the story bible and has no role in this short's exposition.

Avoid premature signals of an evil trap: laughing mascots, bloody balls, sinister PA delivery, skulls, or a demonic portal. The birthday party can be noisy and unpleasant for a hungover adult without becoming a haunted attraction. Once he arrives below, the scale is unsettling, while the route ahead gives him hope.

Do not portray Ethan's lapse as a punishment that he deserves. He misses a moment and then tries to help. His love needs to remain legible, because the larger story depends on the audience supporting that love.

## Make the difficult actions editable

Ball pits combine moving bodies, hands, flexible surfaces, and hundreds of small objects. Do not make the whole disappearance one technically demanding generation. Build it from readable pieces: Sarah is upset; her hand is above the surface; balls settle; Ethan reaches the right place; the place is empty. The exact edit can be established with stills first.

Use the same principle for Ethan's descent. A close view of his shoulder entering the gap can cut to a dark, confined passage, then to a landing. A sound bridge and consistent movement can establish continuity without a model having to transform the birthday room into an enormous new world in one shot.

Keep staff mostly offscreen or cropped at the shoulder. Use incidental background guests without recurring close-ups. During the search, staff clear the pit so Ethan's movement becomes readable and the generation task becomes simpler.

For the first environment reveal, begin with Ethan or the landing surface. Give the audience his attempt to orient himself, then show the full scale. A spectacular establishing image has more effect after we have been unable to see beyond the balls and passage.

## Continuity and sound rules

Ethan's clothes remain intact through this opening, with modest dust or ball-pit grime after the descent. The torn sleeve from the superseded search sequence is not part of this short. He does not visibly age.

Sarah's star is visible before the disappearance, absent from the pit after Ethan retrieves it, and in his pocket until he takes it out below. Her crown goes on Ethan briefly and is left on the party table. Do not carry either prop into shots where it should no longer be visible.

Set a clear party-room layout before making reverse angles. Ethan should reach the same side of the pit where he last saw Sarah. Keep the padded seam in one identified back corner. Avoid having the next shot silently move it to another wall.

Carry the party audio into the pit, then muffle it as Ethan passes the seam. Let it disappear only after he crosses. The new ambience can be spacious and inviting; the absence of the birthday room is the meaningful change. Do not use Sarah's disembodied voice as a new lure in this draft. The only guidance below is the illuminated route and welcome.

## First production test

Start with the threshold rather than a general model demonstration. A 20–30 second test should contain Ethan searching at the padded seam, entering it, landing below, and seeing the first fragment of the Playways. Validate recognizable Ethan, readable direction of travel, consistent clothes, and the change in sound perspective.

Also make a short performance test of Sarah asking him to watch and Ethan becoming distracted. The transition can be spectacular and still fail as a film if their relationship feels mechanical.

After those tests, build a complete timed animatic using approved stills and scratch dialogue. The opening should work before final video production. The planned 35 shots total 7,200 frames at 24 fps, including the title card; final performance and edit trims remain to be selected.
