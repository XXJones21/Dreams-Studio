# Everyone Here Is Happy — Halloween short (design)

Drafted October 31, 2026, with Josh. A three-minute, first-person, 9:16 horror short built from the *Everyone Here Is Happy* opening. This document is the brief for the script, the Blender POV blockouts, and the Pika H3 generations. It sits beside the five-minute [opening short](../opening-short/adaptation-plan.md) and does not replace it.

## Intent

A father loses his daughter in the play structure of a cheap pizza-and-games place, climbs in after her, gets lost, slides into the Playways, and is left facing a task he cannot possibly complete. The film ends in cosmic dread, not hope.

Josh's core image is the transition: frantic searching inside an ordinary play structure, a slide that should not be there, and a crash into an impossibly large ball pit with impossible geometry. Everything before that exists to make the fall land. Everything after it exists to show the size of what he now has to do.

Why now: it's Halloween, and the project already has an opening screenplay, character reference workflows, a blockout-to-previs pipeline, and the new Pika H3 Flex workflow. This short is the first full use of all of them together.

## Decided with Josh

- **Length:** about three minutes.
- **Point of view:** first person for the whole film. We are Dad. We only ever see his hands and forearms.
- **Frame:** 9:16 vertical, matching the inspiration short and Dreams-studio's existing 9:16 pipeline.
- **Opening:** 45–60 seconds establishing a birthday party at a knock-off Chuck E. Cheese.
- **Inciting moment:** Dad sees Sarah being picked on in the ball pit. She runs off crying into the play structure. (This differs from the opening-short screenplay, where she vanishes under the balls.)
- **Search:** Dad climbs into the normal play structure to find her, starts to get lost, and finds what looks like a slide.
- **Fall:** the slide drops him into the Playways. He crashes into an impossibly large ball pit with impossible geometry.
- **Ending:** dread. Cosmic horror. The impossible task set before the protagonist.
- **The Manager has no voice.** No PA announcements, no welcome, no venue voice. Its presence is only in what he sees.
- **Visual reference:** Josh's Playways reference image (see References) and the *Archive In Between* "You're in the Playways" short.

## Story

### Beats and timing

| Time | Beat | What the audience must understand |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:55 | **The party.** Dad at the table, hungover. Sarah drops a paper crown on his head. She taps the table twice: "You say ready." He says "Ready." She jumps the carpet line and checks he saw. His phone buzzes: Lena offering to have Mark collect Sarah. He starts a reply. The knock-off mascot in costume works the room. | Sarah is a specific, loved child. Dad is almost present. The mascot exists. |
| 0:55–1:10 | **The ball pit.** "Dad. Watch this one." "One second." He looks up late: a bigger kid takes Sarah's spot or pushes her. She cries and runs into the play structure entrance. | A small harm he was right there for and didn't catch. |
| 1:10–1:50 | **The search.** He squeezes into the structure (built for children; adult shoulders brush the netting). Net tunnels, padded steps, tube junctions. He glimpses her shoe or blue top turning a corner ahead. He calls "Sarah." The tubes go on longer than the building could hold. The party noise thins behind him. A junction he's sure he passed comes back around. | He is getting lost, and the structure is wrong, but it still looks ordinary. |
| 1:50–2:05 | **The slide.** A dark slide mouth he hasn't seen before. He goes in (or the floor tips him in). Acceleration, light streaks, the tube going far longer than any slide. | The point of no return. |
| 2:05–2:25 | **The crash and reveal.** He bursts out and plunges into balls; sound goes muffled; colored dark. His hands break the surface. Silence where the party was, a low hum. He looks up: the impossible Playways tower (Josh's reference image, reframed vertically). | The scale is beyond anything human. |
| 2:25–3:00 | **The impossible task.** He hauls out onto a ledge. Far off on a net bridge: a small figure in Sarah's blue top, back turned. He shouts her name. Then another blue top in a lit window. Another on a slide. He tilts up: every bridge, tube, and window rising into the haze holds a small figure in her top, all turned away. The giant mascot idol grins over it all, perfectly still. He looks at the yellow star in his hand. Tilt up into a tower with no top. Cut to black. Title: **EVERYONE HERE IS HAPPY**. | He must find one child among endless children he cannot tell apart, in a place without end. |

The timings are animatic targets. If the party plays long, cut it before padding anything else.

### Dialogue

Keep it minimal and natural. The only lines are Sarah's ("You say ready." "Dad. Watch this one."), Dad's ("Ready." "One second." "Sarah."/"Sarah!"), and the phone text, shown as an on-screen insert. There is no Manager voice, narration, or venue PA.

### Props and continuity

- **Yellow star wrist token on Sarah's left wrist** (already part of the Sarah reference workflow). It comes off during the search. Dad finds it in a tube during 1:10–1:50 and pockets it, which confirms she came this way. It is in his hand in the final beat.
- **Paper crown:** goes on Dad at the table, stays on the table.
- **Sarah's blue top** is the identifying garment. At the end it is the thing that multiplies.
- **Dad's sleeves and hands** must stay consistent shot to shot (use the Ethan anchor for costume). They pick up ball-pit grime after the crash, nothing more.
- **The mascot.** The party's knock-off mascot (a cheap costume) and the giant idol in the Playways share the same face. This is an original character, not a copy of Chuck E. Cheese's mouse or any other real mascot. Render 23 (`manager_mascot_idol`) in the Engram project is the starting point for the design.

## Rules carried from the story bible

- **Fingerprints, never a hand.** The Manager appears only as the idol and as places that seem to watch (an empty control booth, camera domes). It never moves, speaks, or appears as a creature.
- **No discrete monster.** If a generation produces a creature, ball worm, or roaming mascot, reject it.
- **No gore, no skulls, no blood-red balls, no demonic portal.** The horror is scale, wrongness, and misrecognition.
- **The party is ordinary.** Loud, sticky, unpleasant for a hungover adult, but not a haunted attraction. The wrongness starts inside the structure, and gradually.
- **Dad's love stays legible.** His lapse is a missed moment, not a punishment he deserves. He acts the instant he notices.

## Goals, non-goals, anti-goals

**Goals**
- The fall into the Playways feels like the floor of reality giving way.
- The last 35 seconds produce dread through scale and repetition: one Sarah, then many, then too many to count.
- A complete, watchable three-minute cut, built on consistent Sarah, consistent Dad hands, and a consistent look.

**Non-goals**
- Explaining the Playways, the Manager, time dilation, or the Archives.
- Showing Sarah's fate or any third-person view.
- Replacing the five-minute opening short.

**Anti-goals** (fail even if it technically works)
- The ending reads as hopeful ("she's just ahead").
- The idol or the multiplying children read as jump-scare monsters rather than indifferent scale.
- The party already feels sinister.
- Sarah looks different from shot to shot before the ending, which would muddy the deliberate misrecognition at the end.

## Look

- **Register:** the "Lane B" look from the Engram visual-dev notes: Discovery Zone candy palette, deep blue ambient dark, warm caged practical lights, teal-and-orange grade, volumetric haze, cathedral vertical scale. Add 1990s film-photo grain to match the *Archive In Between* short.
- **The party** is brighter, flatter, and more fluorescent: an ordinary room. The structure gets dimmer and warmer the deeper he goes. The Playways is the full register.
- **Josh's reference image** is ultrawide. For 9:16, use it as the style and geometry source and reframe vertically. A vertical frame suits the tower and the final tilt-up.

## Production pipeline

1. **Screenplay** (`halloween-short/screenplay-v01.fountain`) from the beats above.
2. **Shot manifest** (`halloween-short/shot-manifest-v01.json`): about 36 shots of about 5 s each, with time range, camera move, references needed, and blockout file.
3. **Blender POV blockouts** in `D:\Comfy\blockout-previs-demo\halloween-playways\` (same pattern as `bank-robbery-chase`). Every camera is Dad's head height and motion: seated at the table, crouched in tubes, sliding, submerged, looking up. Render each shot as a 9:16 clay clip, plus an inverse-depth (disparity) pass if a depth route is used.
4. **Style key first** (Josh's standing preference): one Nano Banana Pro style key per zone (party, structure, Playways), each built from a clay frame plus Josh's reference image.
5. **Per-shot style frames:** clay frame + zone style key (+ Sarah anchor when she is in frame).
6. **Pika H3 Flex ref2va** (`workflows/video_pika_h3_flex_ref2va.json`) at 768×1344, 5 s. Inputs: `ref_video_0` = the blockout clip (camera motion), `ref_image_0..2` = style frame, Sarah reference, location/idol reference. Use the workflow's structured prompt format (subject_definitions / summary / retention_analysis / detailed_description / overall_soundscape). It also generates audio.
7. **Edit:** assemble, replace or sweeten audio where H3's audio is weak, add the phone insert and the title card.

### Spike before committing

The H3 Flex workflow in the repo ships with reference images only; `ref_video_0` is unwired. Before blocking out all 36 shots, run one test. Feed one POV blockout clip (the tube crawl into the slide mouth is a good choice) as `ref_video_0` and check that H3 follows its camera. If it doesn't, fall back to the depth-driven H3 route from `blockout-previs-demo` (`blockout_to_previs.json`) for the camera-critical shots, and use Flex ref2va for the reference-heavy ones.

### Known lessons from the chase test

- Depth for H3 must be inverse depth (disparity), not linear. This matters for the long tower shots.
- H3 follows depth for the camera but follows the prompt for small figures, and it tends to add extras. State exact child counts in party shots. For the final multiplication shot, the extras are the goal, but each figure must hold the blue top and back-turned pose.
- Phantom extras usually come from the style frame, so fix them there, not in H3.
