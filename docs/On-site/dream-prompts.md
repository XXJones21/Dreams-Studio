# Dream prompts

Prompts for the Dreams Studio form: three dreams that ran during development, plus an alternative scene for each. Paste each block into the field with the same name. They're written for **15 s** clips; each clip's action is timed to fill 15 seconds.

## What made these prompts work

- **Put a cast sheet in the Style.** Every clip gets the Style, so describe the hands, weapon count, and recurring creatures there, once. This stopped extra swords and creatures that changed look between clips.
- **Write each clip as stage directions.** Numbered or timed beats, about one beat per 4 to 5 seconds. Too little action and the model fills the time by undoing it, or by adding extra creatures.
- **Say how many, and say "no others".** "Exactly three skeletons… no other skeletons ever appear."
- **End each clip on a stated end state.** The next clip starts on the last frame, so its prompt should open where the last one ended.
- **Describe characters by how they look, not by name.** The models don't reliably know game names; a visual description holds up.
- **Watch for words that turn into people.** "Red shirt sleeves" produced a stranger in a red shirt walking ahead of the camera. Say "the only part of me ever visible is…" and put "a second person" in Avoid.

## Metroid: the corridor and the cavern

Ran on Oct 12 as the first film made through the Dreams Studio form, with **Generate** keyframe and 15 s clips.

**Style**

```
First-person POV, one single continuous handheld take with no cuts; the camera is my own eyes behind a helmet visor, and I am alone. The only part of me ever visible is my arms in the lower frame: my right forearm is a bulky, scuffed, faded orange-red powered armor arm that ends in a round cannon barrel instead of a hand; my left hand, in a matching dented armored glove, is braced under it. There is exactly one cannon. No other person ever appears. A still from a 1980s live-action science-fiction film in the style of Alien, Aliens and Dragonslayer, shot on Kodak 35mm film stock with practical sets, miniatures and real physical materials: wet black rock, corroded bronze pipework, dripping water, steam, old bone. Color grade: muted, slightly faded colors; cool steel-blue haze and steam filling the air; soft warm amber glow from distant lava that glows rather than burns; lifted milky blacks, low contrast; diffusion filter bloom and halation around every light; soft focus falloff; visible film grain. Lonely, grim and ominous.
```

**Keyframe scene**

```
A long, narrow alien corridor deep underground, carved from ribbed black rock and fused with ancient corroded bronze pipes and grates. Steam drifts low across the floor, and water drips from the ceiling. Faint amber light glows through cracks in the walls. Far ahead at the end of the corridor, a round glowing blue hatch door is set into the rock. My right arm cannon is raised and pointed down the corridor, my left hand braced under it.
```

**Avoid**

```
Avoid: cartoon, anime, illustration, painting, 3D render, CGI look, video game graphics, HUD text, numbers, crosshairs, health bars, UI overlays, glossy plastic armor, toy, neon colors, oversaturated colors, crisp digital sharpness, high contrast, crushed pure-black shadows, modern teal-and-orange grade, third-person view, a second armored figure, extra arms, two cannons.
```

**Clip 1**

```
I am alone in the corridor; the only creature in this clip is one small crawler. The motion is slow and heavy, one action at a time. 0–3 s: I step slowly forward, the arm cannon sweeping left, pausing, then sweeping right; a cracked pipe beside me hisses out a jet of steam that drifts across the frame. 3–6 s: On the left wall a single crawler creature the size of a cat, with a hard spiked orange shell and short scuttling legs, creeps along the rock; it stops, and a beat later its head turns toward me. 6–9 s: I swing the cannon toward it and hold for a beat; the barrel glows from inside, then fires one bright yellow-white energy shot in a straight line; the crawler bursts into sparks and its shell clatters down the wall, bouncing twice before it settles. 9–13 s: I walk on past it as the corridor narrows; water drips onto the cannon; the lights in the cracks flicker, and a low, distant growl echoes from somewhere far ahead. 13–15 s: I stop in front of the round glowing blue hatch door at the end of the corridor; it fills the center of the frame, humming softly. **Clip 2**

```

## Zelda: Gohma's lair

Ran as runs 5 to 7. This is the latest version: the run 5 Style and Avoid with the v2.3 clip actions. The sword is in the left hand and the shield on the right, because the hero is left-handed.

**Style**

```
First-person POV, one single continuous handheld take with no cuts; the camera is my own eyes and I am alone. In the lower frame I always see exactly two hands: my left hand, in a brown leather gauntlet, holds one short steel sword; my right forearm carries a round wooden shield with a red spiral emblem. There is only one sword, and the shield never leaves my right arm. The skeletons always look the same: adult-sized human skeletons with normal anatomical proportions, weathered brown-stained bone, cracked skulls with faint red embers deep in the eye sockets, scraps of rotted cloth and dirt clinging to them. Photorealistic early-1990s live-action dark fantasy film still, shot on 35mm film with a real lens, practical sets and real physical materials: wet root, packed earth, old bone, rusted iron. Natural textures and real-world scale. Torchlight against cold green-blue darkness, deep shadows, heavy film grain, drifting dust, grim and ominous.
```

**Keyframe scene**

```
A deep underground cavern beneath an ancient giant tree. Thick twisted roots form the walls and ceiling, hung with cobwebs and patches of faintly glowing green moss. Torches burn in iron brackets on the roots. The dirt floor in front of me is cracked open, and a bony skeletal hand is clawing up out of the earth. My sword is held forward in my left hand, my shield raised on my right arm.
```

**Avoid**

```
Avoid: cartoon, anime, illustration, painting, 3D render, CGI look, video game graphics, toy, plastic, claymation, chibi or bobblehead proportions, oversized heads, cute faces, glossy surfaces, oversaturated colors, extra hands, two swords.
```

**Clip 1**

```
There are exactly three skeletons in this scene, and the cavern behind them stays empty; no other skeletons ever appear. The action is slow and fills the whole shot. In order: 1. The ground cracks open in front of me and a skeleton on the left slowly drags itself up out of the dirt. 2. A taller skeleton claws its way up in the center. 3. A third skeleton pulls itself up on the right. 4. Soil and roots fall from their ribs as all three stand up, heads twitching toward me. 5. They stagger toward me one slow step at a time, the center one raising its claws, while I back up with my sword and shield raised. 6. They stop close in front of me, swaying, jaws clacking. ```

**Clip 2**

```
Exactly three skeletons stand in front of me: one on the left, a taller one in the center, one on the right. No other skeletons. In order: 1. The center skeleton lunges and claws at my shield; I block, then swing my sword and smash it apart: its skull flies off and rolls away, its ribcage cracks into pieces, and its arm and leg bones drop separately into the dirt. 2. I lower my sword and look down at the floor: no whole skeletons remain, only a loose scatter of separate bones (skulls, broken ribs, leg bones, finger bones) strewn across the dirt like a dropped bundle of sticks. Nothing moves; I breathe hard. Sound: claws scraping wood, steel cracking bone, bones clattering onto dirt, my heavy breathing.
```

**Clip 3**

```

The loose bones twitch and slide across the dirt toward each other; the skulls roll back into place and half-built ribcages start lifting off the ground. 2. I look up: the roots of the ceiling crack and dirt pours down, and a giant spider-like creature clinging upside down to the roots lets go. 3.
